Mona Lisa - Leonardo da Vinci

Widely considered the most famous work by the most famous artist to ever live, Leonardo da Vinci’s Mona Lisa features a smile so realistic it draws thousands to the Louvre daily. This half-height portrait, believed to be of Lisa Gherardini, became a global phenomenon after it was stolen in 1911 by an Italian employee named Peruggia, who believed the masterpiece belonged in Italy.

Recovered and returned to France, it now holds the record for the highest insurance valuation, estimated at over $800 million in today’s currency.

The Scream - Edvard Munch

Edvard Munch’s ‘The Scream’, originally titled ‘The Scream of Nature’, has become a universal symbol of anxiety. Munch created four versions of this haunting image, inspired by an evening walk where the sky suddenly turned "blood red."

As his friends walked on, Munch stood trembling, sensing what he described as an infinite scream passing through nature, captured forever in the swirling colors of the fjord and sky.

The Last Supper - Leonardo da Vinci

Residing in Milan, Italy, "The Last Supper" portrays the dramatic final meal shared by Jesus and his apostles. Leonardo chose to use oil rather than traditional fresco techniques, allowing him to work slowly and refine the emotional expressions of the figures.

Because of this experimental method, the wall required special sealing with gesso and pitch. Though it has suffered from environmental wear over centuries, it remains a pillar of Western art history.

Girl with a Pearl Earring - Vermeer

Painted around 1665, this work is known as a "tronie"—a Dutch term for a character study rather than a formal portrait. Modern science has shown that the dark background was once a lush green glaze that has since faded to black.

Despite the changes over time, the subject's gaze and the luminous pearl continue to make this one of the most popular and timeless paintings in the world.

The Birth of Venus - Sandro Botticelli

Painted in the mid-1480s, this masterpiece depicts the Roman goddess Venus arriving at the shore fully grown. It was commissioned by the powerful Medici banking family of Florence, who were massive patrons of the arts during the Renaissance.

Alongside its sister piece, the 'Primavera', it remains one of the most iconic depictions of mythological figures in art history.

Sunflowers - Vincent van Gogh

Van Gogh painted twelve different sunflower works during his time in Paris and Arles. While the earlier Paris versions showed flowers lying flat, the Arles paintings featured them standing tall in vases.

Van Gogh was a pioneer in using newly available, vibrant yellow oil paints. He embraced these bright shades to bring a unique energy and warmth to his work that still resonates with viewers today.
